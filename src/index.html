Cookie Clicker is a game by Orteil in which cookies buy items that bake more cookies. Four decisions return through the whole game. They are: what to buy next, how many cookies to keep for golden cookies, how to spend heavenly chips, and when to ascend. Each decision has a rule that is exact in a small model of the game.

No rule is proved for the whole game. The mod Cookie Monster+ plays by these rules, and the Tools page installs it.

{{in-paper: Theorem 2.1 | 3 ; Example 2.2 | 4 ; Theorem 2.4 | 5}}

The income \(c\) is the number of cookies that you receive in each second. Item \(i\) costs \(p_i\) cookies and adds \(\Delta_i\) to the income. You want two items, \(A\) and \(B\), and the bank holds neither price.

With \(A\) first, you save \(p_A\) at the income \(c\), and then \(p_B\) at the income \(c + \Delta_A\). The two orders end with the same income and an empty bank, so the faster order is the better one.

Rule 1

Buy \(A\) first if \(E_A < E_B\), where

\[ E_i = \frac{p_i}{c} + \frac{p_i}{\Delta_i}. \]

The first part of \(E_i\) is the time to save for the item. The second part is the payback time, in which the item earns its price. At an income of 100, an item of 1,000 cookies that adds 10 has \(E = 10 + 100 = 110\) seconds. An item of 5,000 cookies that adds 80 has \(E = 50 + 62.5 = 112.5\) seconds, so it goes second.

With three items the rule can lose time. Start with an income of 1 and an empty bank. The items A, B and C cost 3, 5 and 6 cookies and add 1, 4 and 6. The rule buys A, B, C in \(13/2\) seconds. The order B, C, A takes \(356/55\) seconds, which is 0.4% less.

For a fixed list of items, a method that goes through the sets of bought items finds the fastest order. In the game each purchase changes other prices and gains, so Rule 1 is an estimate there.

If the bank holds each price but not the two together, buy the item with the larger \(\Delta_i\) first.

{{in-paper: Theorem 3.1 | 6 ; Lemma 3.2 | 7}}

A golden cookie appears at random times, and a click on it gives one of several effects. The effect Lucky pays

\[ \min(0.15\,b,\ 900\,c) + 13 \]

cookies. Here \(b\) is the bank, and \(c\) is the production: the cookies that your buildings bake in each second. Up to a bank of 6,000 seconds of production, the payout is 15% of the bank. So each cookie in the bank earns a part of each Lucky payout.

After a golden cookie the game waits 5 minutes, and the next one appears at a random time in the following 10 minutes. The mean wait is 447 seconds. The upgrades Lucky day and Serendipity each halve the two times, and with the two upgrades the mean wait is 121 seconds.

About 42% of the golden cookies give Lucky. So Lucky arrives at the rate \(\lambda = 0.42 / 121\) in each second, and a banked cookie earns \(0.15\,\lambda\) cookies in each second. A cookie that you spend on item \(i\) earns \(\Delta_i / p_i\).

Rule 2

Up to a bank of 6,000 seconds of production, a purchase increases the expected income if \(p_i / \Delta_i < \theta\), where

\[ \theta = \frac{1}{0.15\,\lambda}. \]

With the two upgrades, \(\theta\) is 1,915 seconds, about 32 minutes. Without them it is 7,060 seconds. Early in a run the payback times are seconds, so spend each cookie. Late in a run they are hours, so keep the bank at 6,000 seconds of production. The rule compares two incomes at one moment, and it does not give the fastest order of purchases.

{{in-paper: Theorem 4.1 | 8 ; Example 4.2 | 9}}

You can ascend at any time. An ascension ends the run: you lose the bank, the buildings and the upgrades. In return you get prestige, and each new level of prestige gives one heavenly chip. Chips buy upgrades that stay through each later ascension.

Upgrade \(j\) costs \(p_j\) chips and multiplies the speed of each later run by \(m_j\). A higher speed brings new chips faster, so an early upgrade shortens the wait for each later one.

Rule 3

If each chip costs the same effort, buy the upgrades in the order of decreasing

\[ \rho_j = \frac{1 - 1/m_j}{p_j}. \]

To see why, exchange two adjacent upgrades \(i\) and \(j\) of an order. The other waits stay the same, and the total wait is shorter if \(\rho_i < \rho_j\).

The rule can tell you to save chips. Upgrade X costs 10 chips and multiplies the speed by 1.05. Upgrade Y costs 100 chips and multiplies the speed by 3. Then \(\rho_X = 0.0048\) is less than \(\rho_Y = 0.0067\). So you keep the first 10 chips and wait for Y.

In the game a chip costs more effort at a higher prestige. The paper has an example in which Rule 3 then loses time. So in the game the rule is an estimate.

{{in-paper: Theorem 5.3 | 10 ; Table 2 | 12 ; Proposition 5.4 | 12}}

Let \(A\) be the number of cookies that all your runs have baked. The prestige is

\[ P = \sqrt[3]{A / 10^{12}}. \]

After the upgrade Legacy, each level of prestige adds 1% to the production of each later run. So late in the game, the speed of a run is in proportion to the prestige.

Suppose that a run bakes cookies in proportion to \(t^\beta\), where \(t\) is its age. The number \(\beta\) is the growth exponent of the run. To estimate it, multiply the income by the age of the run, and divide by the cookies of the run.

Rule 4

Ascend when \(A\) is \(v^\star\) times its value at the start of the run. The number \(v^\star\) is the one solution above 1 of

\[ \frac{\beta}{3}\left(1 - \frac{1}{v}\right) = \frac{1 - v^{-\gamma/3}}{\gamma}, \qquad \gamma = \frac{3}{\beta} - 1. \]

The prestige grows by the cube root of the factor. At \(\beta = 1.5\) this is the golden ratio, 1.618.

A known rule says: ascend when the present rate of gain falls to the mean rate of the run. That rule is correct only at \(\beta = 3\), because later runs take more or less time. At \(\beta = 6\) it gives the factor 397, and Rule 4 gives 60.9.

Early in the game the prestige changes the speed very little. If all runs have the same speed, one long run is the fastest way to bake a number of cookies. So an early ascension only loses time.

{{in-paper: Proposition 5.2 | 10}}

Each achievement adds 4% milk, and the kitten upgrades change milk into production. With kitten factors \(k_i\), milk \(\mu\) and a milk multiplier \(m\), production has the factor \(\prod_i (1 + k_i m \mu)\). In a game with eight kitten upgrades, 952% milk and \(m = 1.1\), one more achievement multiplies production by 1.0214.

Some achievements cost time. Suppose that your goal is \(H\) seconds away. An action stops production for \(D\) seconds and then multiplies it by \(M\). With the action the goal is \(D + H/M\) seconds away.

Rule 5

The action saves time if

\[ D < H\left(1 - \frac{1}{M}\right). \]

With the goal 77 hours away, one achievement repays a pause of up to 1.61 hours, and two repay 3.19 hours. The achievements Neverclick and Hardcore need a Born again run, which starts without prestige and without upgrades. If that run takes 4 hours, it saves time when the goal is more than 97 hours away.

Godzamok is a god of the Pantheon. When you sell buildings, he gives a buff to click power: a bonus for 10 seconds. Each building sold adds 1% in the first slot, 0.5% in the second and 0.25% in the third. Sales during the buff add to it, and they do not make it longer.

A sale returns 25% of the price of the next copy, and the next copy costs 1.15 times the copy that you sell. So a sale returns \(1.15 \times 25\% = 28.75\%\) of the price of the copy. To sell buildings and buy them back costs 71.25% of their price, and production does not change.

Suppose that the mod sells and buys back \(U\) buildings in each of the 300 frames of a buff. In the first slot the buff increases by \(0.01\,U\) in each frame, and its mean is

\[ 1 + 0.01\,U\,\frac{300 + 1}{2} \approx 1 + 1.5\,U. \]

With 2,200 buildings in each frame, the buff ends at ×6,601 and its mean is ×3,312. The mouse upgrades make a click worth 15% of production, so 20 clicks in a second bring 3 times production. With the buff they bring about 9,900 times production.

The Grandmapocalypse has three stages. At stage \(\ell\), each golden cookie is a wrath cookie with chance \(\ell/3\), and wrinklers appear. A wrinkler eats a part of production. When you burst it, it returns 1.1 times the cookies that it ate.

With \(n\) wrinklers, the share \(s = n/20\) of production withers, and each wrinkler eats that whole share. So wrinklers multiply the income from production by

\[ W(n) = 1 - s + 1.1\,s\,n. \]

Ten wrinklers give \(W = 6\), and twelve give 8.32.

Some effects of a wrath cookie take cookies. An Elder Pledge stops the wrath, and the wrinklers go away. An estimate for twelve wrinklers gives the income \(62.8\,c + 8.5\,k\) with wrath and \(8.7\,c + 11.8\,k\) with a pledge. Here \(c\) is production and \(k\) is the income from clicks. So the pledge earns more when \(k\) is more than about 16 times \(c\), as it is with Godzamok.

The four numbers of the estimate come from the shares of the golden cookie effects. They have no proof, and they are not in the paper.

In the Grimoire, magic \(m\) fills at the rate \(\kappa\sqrt{m}\), with a fixed number \(\kappa\). To fill from \(a\) to \(a + C\) takes

\[ \frac{2}{\kappa}\left(\sqrt{a + C} - \sqrt{a}\right) \]

seconds, and this time decreases when \(a\) increases. So cast a spell at full magic. With a maximum of 72 and a spell that costs 53, the spell is then ready each 23 minutes. From empty magic it is ready each 40 minutes.

In the Garden, a new plant grows by mutation in an empty tile next to its parent plants. Use every second tile, like the dark squares of a chessboard. Plant parent A on the dark squares of the columns 1, 3 and 5. Plant parent B on the dark squares of the columns 2, 4 and 6. Then each empty tile touches the two parents, on each plot of at least 2 by 2 tiles.

In the Stock Market, the price of good \(i\) moves around the resting value \(R_i = 10 + 10\,i + (\ell - 1)\). Here \(i\) counts the goods from 0, and \(\ell\) is the level of the Bank building. A purchase costs up to 20% extra. So a purchase below \(0.6\,R_i\) and a sale at \(R_i\) return at least \(1/(0.6 \times 1.2) - 1 = 38.9\%\). The wait for these prices can be long.

The offices of the Stock Market open three loans. A loan multiplies production for a time, and then it divides production for a longer time. If the run ends before the boost ends, the penalty does not come. So take a loan near the end of a run.
